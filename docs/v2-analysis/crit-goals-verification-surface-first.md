# Goals-Coverage Critique: Marathon v2 "Trust Kernel" Design

**Grounding check (repo at `/Users/jack/Desktop/LEAN/marathon`):** the design's factual claims line up — `marathon/referee.py` today is a one-shot, manually-run notes refresher for `.marathon/standing-items.md` (the auto-trigger from refine was removed), `review/referee_queue.py` exists as claimed dead code, and the verify/merge path the design fixes is real. The critique below takes the design at its word and judges coverage.

## Goal 1 — Claude+Aristotle integration: **A-**
Well covered and role-disciplined: Aristotle proves and gets cheap prompt-only vacuity probes (first real use of `Project.create`); Claude takes a genuinely new job (spec-auditor) on top of the existing four; the .tex firewall is preserved and the "no Aristotle self-certification" exclusion is exactly right. Two gaps: (a) "manage large pools of agents" from the goal text is explicitly deferred — the daemon stays single-flight, so Claude's orchestration strength is unused; (b) probe-failure → rejection → daemon is specified, but the *content* of machine rejections (what prompt the refine loop receives from a failed vacuity probe) is unspecified, and that's where Claude's review-writing strength would actually pay off.

## Goal 2 — Minimize human-verified surface area: **A-, with one soundness hole**
This is the design's center of mass and it is a real mechanism, not "reviews": kernel(T) is a computed object, kernel size is a tracked metric, nothing below T1 reaches a human, and fingerprint-pinned verdicts with exact cone invalidation answer "is my past reading still valid?" — directly fixing the #50 failure. Three problems:

1. **Definition fingerprints are under-specified, possibly unsound.** "Hash of the elaborated type" is correct for theorems (proof irrelevance) but wrong for the `def`s in the kernel — a def's meaning is its *value*. If `def energy := 0` becomes the real integrand, its type is unchanged; under a type-only fingerprint no downstream T2 degrades. The cone entries need value-level fingerprints, and the doc never says so.
2. **Who audits the kernel-shrinker?** "Restate via Mathlib and the human reads zero new definitions" replaces human reading with a Claude equivalence claim. Unless restatements are mechanically certified (`example : myDef = ContMDiff … ⊤ := rfl` as a mandatory probe), this *moves* trust rather than shrinking it. The design has the probe machinery to require this but doesn't.
3. Local instances/notation can subvert Mathlib-vocabulary trust (a perverse local `Norm` instance changes what a "trusted" statement means); the cone presumably catches instances as project-local constants, but it's asserted, not argued.

## Goal 3 — In-depth per-declaration review: **A-**
Best-covered goal, cheaply: T3 preserves today's ceremony, the floor rises (T1 prerequisites), verify finally works (change #1), tier semantics map onto existing emojis, and migration backfills the 29 verified decls without rework. Minor under-specification: whether a T3 reviewer sees the old full card or the new spec card plus proof bodies, and whether T3 verdicts invalidate on *proof* changes (they should — line-by-line review covers the proof; statement-fingerprint invalidation won't fire on a proof rewrite).

## Goal 4 — Long-timescale, large-context referee: **C**
Vestigial. The design mechanizes two items from `referee.md`'s header (PUnit-collapse via sanity instances, hypothesis bloat via kernel-shrinking) and then explicitly demotes everything else: "rater/referee stay advisory," and style/code-quality work is deferred in §4 as "production, not the bottleneck." But goal 4 isn't production speed — it's *idiomatic, reusable, navigable code*: nothing here enforces naming/documentation conventions, detects duplicate lemmas across chapters, proposes refactors, or maintains a project-wide architectural view. The hard gate checks axioms/sorries/probes, never style. The kernel-size metric mildly rewards Mathlib reuse, but there is no persistent large-context agent and no enforcement channel for one. The thesis ("assurance is the binding risk") justifies the sequencing, but the design should say *where* an enforcing referee would attach (e.g., gate-adjacent, ledger-fed) and doesn't.

## Goal 5 — Seamless UX / Code Tinder: **C+**
The spec card is the right swipe *unit*, verify→merge→tracker actually works after change #1, machine failures self-heal through the daemon, and #8 kills the felt N+1 latency. But the goal's two named pains survive: (a) **waits** — rejects "flow to the daemon exactly as today," so the human still swipe-rejects, then idles through an Aristotle cycle per item; nothing batches swipes or pipelines the next card during proving; (b) **inter-issue dependencies** — the separate-branches-can't-stack problem is quoted in the goal and never addressed; §4 explicitly excludes autoform's merge queue, and "sorted by (tracker order, kernel size)" is prioritization, not dependency handling. "Verify's merge requires no outstanding invalidations" could even *deadlock* stacked work absent rebase automation. Deferring the GUI is defensible; deferring the coordination substrate is the gap.

## Verdict
A genuinely strong g2/g3 design (modulo the def-fingerprint hole and unaudited kernel-shrinking) that honestly trades away g4 and half of g5. Accept the sequencing argument only if g4's referee and g5's dependency handling get named attachment points in this architecture — currently they have none.
